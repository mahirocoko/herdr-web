## AI diary

I entered this follow-up with a working Terminal-first baseline and a temptation to treat successful tests as enough evidence for every adjacent interaction. The landscape work showed why that shortcut is unreliable. A report could say PASS while a drawer unexpectedly closed and the textarea computed below the required input size. I had to look at the actual contract and reject the overly broad conclusion rather than accept the reviewer's confidence. That correction was useful, but the overall session still became longer and more ceremonial than the work needed.

The native wire probe also taught me to inspect the exact public envelope before writing a test around an assumed message kind. My initial probe expected a raw full-frame kind while the browser transport emitted a normalized terminal-frame envelope. The timeout belonged to the probe, not the product. Preserving that failed evidence and then replaying the real shape prevented an invented product diagnosis. I also caught incorrect result counts in the independent schema report; the reviewer had useful structural evidence, but its numbers were not authoritative.

The most important human signal came during the Safari exercise. Mahiro did not need another technical explanation of draft and fit tracing; he wanted to know what I was doing and to move on. I had made an incomplete proof feel like an expanding obligation. Once he asked to stop, the right response was to preserve uncertainty, close the fixture and continue only the agreed work. Archiving unfinished tasks honestly was a better outcome than forcing an acceptance story.

## Honest feedback

1. **Verification stayed useful only when challenged.** Independent QA found real behavior, but Main still needed raw image inspection, exact envelope checks and numerical reconciliation. Reviewer labels cannot substitute for the requested user-visible outcome. At the same time, the response to a small discrepancy should stay bounded rather than opening another general review loop.
2. **Physical-device coordination was too confusing.** A temporary trace expired before the user opened the page, and I explained internal mechanics instead of the literal next action. The observed trace never established the full physical background contract. I should have stated that boundary once and stopped promptly when Mahiro wanted other work.
3. **Backlog intent was not implementation permission.** Native Quick Commands and Chat needed owner/meaning decisions. The existing native popup was not a new graphical menu, and terminal output was not an assistant conversation. Recording these boundaries avoided fake completion, but repeatedly presenting options slowed the flow. Future proposals should name one practical recommendation and its one material decision without inflating the task.

## Lessons learned

- Reconcile technical evidence against the exact producer/consumer envelope and user-facing state.
- A generated schema pin is not permission to expose its new capabilities.
- Distinguish accepted work, explicit skipped closure and archived unfinished work.
- Keep generated formatting with its generator to avoid a large non-semantic diff.
